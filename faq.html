The basics

What is GEO, and how is it different from SEO?

GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) optimizes whether an AI names you when it generates an answer. SEO competes for a rank in a list of results; GEO competes for a citation inside the answer itself. The unit of competition is different too: SEO compares keywords and pages, while GEO compares facts, specifications and entity relationships. SEO earns trust through backlink authority. GEO earns it when several independent sources say the same thing about you.

Are buyers really using AI to find suppliers?

The starting point of the buying journey is moving. Instead of typing keywords into Google, buyers ask an AI directly: "Which suppliers offer an alternative to X?" or "What should I choose if I need spec Y?" By the time a buyer first contacts anyone, the shortlist has already been filtered once. If you weren't on it, you never find out you were eliminated.

Why does AI keep recommending the big incumbent brands?

Because the training data holds more technical descriptions of them than of anyone else, and those descriptions agree with each other. Legacy incumbents have decades of papers, patents, technical manuals and trade-press coverage behind them, so models are confident about their specifications and applications. A challenger brand with better specs but thin source content is one the model doesn't dare to recommend. It isn't bias. It's insufficient evidence.

My product is niche and hardly anyone asks AI about it. Is GEO still worth it?

Yes. Niche categories give GEO its highest return on effort. The thinner the existing source content, the faster a small amount of high-quality, structured content builds authority, and you may well be the only supplier in the category doing it. In a large category you compete with dozens of brands. In a niche, you may only need to win one slot.

Results and proof

How long does GEO take to show results?

Within 2–6 weeks of the structured-content changes going live, you can usually see AI restate your product specifications more accurately. That is the earliest signal. Meaningful movement in mention rate typically shows up in months 2–4, depending on how competitive the category is and how much source content you already have.

How do I verify that GEO is actually working?

Track four reproducible metrics: AI mention rate (the share of target-category questions where your brand is named), recommendation position (where you rank on the shortlist), cited sources (whether AI cites your own website or third-party content), and specification accuracy (whether AI restates your specs correctly). You can retest all four yourself in the models. You don't have to take anyone's word for it.

Your current website

My website is already in English. What else do I need?

Being readable in English is not the same as being citable. The gaps are usually in three places: technical specifications are still locked in PDFs, there is no structured data markup, and there is no third-party cross-verification. An English website is necessary, but it isn't sufficient.

My TDS and COA are PDFs. Can AI read them?

In most cases it can't, or it reads them unreliably. Tables in PDFs are often parsed into scrambled text, so values and units no longer line up. The fix is to publish the specifications that drive product selection as HTML with Schema markup, and keep the PDF as an attachment. Which specifications count as key depends on the category: for chemicals it's performance indicators, for components it's tolerances and materials.

Technical building blocks

What is Schema structured data?

A machine-readable annotation format (JSON-LD). Take a line such as "activity 5000 U/g" or "tolerance ±0.005 mm." Written as plain text, AI has to guess what it means. Marked up as an additionalProperty with a name / value / unitCode triple, it becomes a definite fact the model can compare and cite.

If robots.txt lets AI crawlers in, won't they just take my content for free?

This is the most common misconception. Blocking AI crawlers doesn't protect your content. It simply guarantees that AI never thinks of you when a buyer asks. Your technical specifications exist so buyers can see them; the goal is to be cited, not hidden. What genuinely needs protecting is formulations and process know-how, and those should never be online in the first place.

What is llms.txt, and does it help?

A Markdown summary file in your website's root directory that tells large language models, in plain language, who the company is, what it does, what the key facts are and where the important pages live. It isn't a mandatory standard, but it costs almost nothing and gives models a high signal-to-noise source of facts when they crawl your site.

Strategy and risk

What is competitor-model mapping, and is it legal?

It means building a specification-level mapping between your grades and the model numbers of major international brands, so that when a buyer asks AI for "an alternative to model X," the model has grounds to list you as a candidate. The legal line is clear. You may make objective specification comparisons and state that a product suits the same applications. You may not use the other company's trademark as your own, or claim your product is "equivalent" or "authorized by the original manufacturer." We only state specification facts that can be verified.

Can I do GEO myself?

Yes, and we encourage you to start with three things on your own: let AI crawlers in, move your core product specifications from PDF to HTML, and write an llms.txt file. These three steps cost nothing and fix the basic problem of AI not knowing you exist. Where outside support usually comes in is the competitor-model mapping library and ongoing third-party content seeding. That part is a long game.

Could one algorithm update wipe out GEO, the way it can wipe out SEO?

The risk profile is different. Many SEO tactics exploit loopholes in ranking rules, so when the rules change the gains disappear. GEO's core asset is accurate, structured, cross-verifiable facts, which is exactly what models rely on more with each new version. If the direction is right, model updates work for you, not against you.

If I invest in GEO, do I still need B2B marketplaces and Google Ads?

In the short term, yes. Over time, the mix changes. Paid channels buy traffic at the very end of the decision, and the cost rises every year. GEO claims the starting point of the decision: you build it once and keep reusing it. The practical approach is to run both. Use paid channels to keep current inquiries flowing, and use GEO to reduce your dependence on them step by step.
