Every piece, about 15 minutes

Post-publish indexing SOP

Writing the content is only half the job. Skip these nine steps and AI may not read it for months. Run through them after every publish; it takes about 15 minutes.

Playbook ref. #69

  1. Confirm the structured data is live

    Q&A pages use FAQPage, how-to guides use HowTo, long-form articles use Article/TechArticle. Run Google's Rich Results Test and fix any errors before publishing.

  2. Add the page to sitemap.xml

    Every new page must appear in the sitemap, with a priority of 0.7–0.9 depending on importance.

  3. Update llms.txt

    Add one line to the page index with a one-sentence summary of what the page covers. This is the table of contents for language models.

  4. Build three internal links

    Every new page must be linked from at least three existing pages, and must link out itself. Orphan pages are almost never crawled.

  5. Submit to search engines

    Submit the URL in Google Search Console, and submit it in Bing Webmaster Tools at the same time.

  6. Syndicate once off-site

    Rewrite the core argument and post it on LinkedIn or an industry community, with a link to the original. AI won't trust a claim that has only one source; it needs corroboration.

  7. Test it in four models

    3–7 days after publishing, ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude and Gemini the core question of the piece, once each, and check whether the piece gets cited.

  8. Log it in the publishing register

    Record the title, URL, publish date, structured data type, where it was syndicated and the date it was first cited.

  9. Re-test after 30 days

    Ask the same questions again and compare citations. If the piece still isn't cited, work out whether the problem is content quality or crawling.

Every piece

Page-to-conversion rules

Content can't just be content. Every piece needs a clear next step. Without one, readers leave when they finish and the piece was written for nothing.

Playbook ref. #70

Mid-article

Soft hook

Halfway through, add one relevant prompt that doesn't interrupt reading.

Template: "Want to know how AI describes your category right now? Get an audit report in 3 business days."

End of article

Primary CTA

The main conversion point of the piece. Use the CTA band block, the visually heaviest element on the page.

Template: the headline promises an outcome, the body states the deliverable and timing, and the button always reads "Get your free AI visibility audit."

Side rail and floating

Always on

The contact rail on the right and the chat button at bottom right stay visible on every page. You don't need to add them page by page.

Note: on mobile they move to the bottom of the screen. Don't stack other overlays on top.

Three hard rules

  1. One primary CTA per piece. Pushing the audit, a white paper and "message us on WhatsApp" at the same time means pushing nothing.
  2. The CTA must fit the content. An article on robots.txt that ends with the audit report works. One that ends with Plans is a jump.
  3. Give value before asking for contact details. A lead-capture popup at the top of an article will wreck your bounce rate.
Pre-publish check

Content quality red lines

Ten rules. If any one of them fails, the piece doesn't ship. What we sell is content AI is confident enough to cite, so our own content has to hold up to scrutiny first.

Playbook ref. #72

Facts and accuracy

  • Specs must be verifiable: every number traces to a source (client TDS, published standards, test records)
  • No fabricated case studies: client cases without permission are always anonymized, and they must have really happened
  • No overstated results: ranges stay ranges, estimates say "about," and nothing is "guaranteed" or "certain"
  • Neutral competitor information: state only publicly verifiable spec comparisons; no disparagement and no misuse of trademarks
  • Date-stamp what ages: give the date of any data you cite, and update descriptions of AI model capabilities as versions change

Structure and expression

  • Question first, answer first: lead with the conclusion, then expand, because AI extracts the opening
  • One idea per paragraph: AI struggles to split long paragraphs into accurate citations
  • Spell out terms on first use: "GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)," then the short form afterward
  • No keyword stuffing: abnormal keyword density gets content judged as low quality and pushed down
  • Match the variables table: brand name, pricing and numbers all follow the standard forms in #58

The easiest one to break

A spec that's off by one digit. Heat resistance written as 15 °C instead of 150 °C. Once AI picks that up, it won't just skip citing you; it may flag your brand as an unreliable source. Have someone technical review every spec before publishing. This step is not optional.
